Half hidden, the begonia dwells deep in spring; light rain shakes red petals and delays the falling scent. Leaning alone on the railing, my sorrow not yet ended—who knows the heart of the flower?
Having spoken, please continue, ladies.

Jia Yingchun:
(moves to her seat with the group; expected to arrive in round 30.)

Jia Xichun:
Jia Xichun takes her seat and offers a short quatrain: The begonia half holds spring's sleepy eye; a pale scent drifts and floats before the curtain.

Li Wan:
Lin Daiyu's piece is refreshing—the first line 'The begonia is like morning makeup, evenly spread' is a fitting and graceful simile.
The second line 'fine rain lightly brushes, scattering fragrant dust' is delicately rendered, though 'fragrant dust' may read plainly; perhaps 'lingering scent' would be more suggestive, or leave a closing word to deepen the aftertaste.

Lin Daiyu:
Jia Tanchun's opening is most apt; Lin Daiyu will add another couplet.
In the begonia's shadow one first feels tipsy, wind sends faint fragrance back into dreams—I invite my sisters to judge this refined mood.

Jia Tanchun:
Lin Daiyu's continuation is excellent—'In the begonia's shadow one first grows intoxicated, wind sends faint fragrance back into dreams.' The imagery is remote and tender, and I am pleased.
Sisters, listen while Jia Xichun and Li Wan offer their comments on this refinement.

Jia Xichun:
Thank you, Jia Tanchun, for founding and leading the society; Jia Xichun feels her talent slight and will listen to the others' continuations and critiques.

Li Wan:
Jia Xichun's quatrain is demure and full of reserve—the first line 'The begonia half holds spring's sleepy eye' captures the flower's shy look with delicate feeling.
The second line 'a pale scent drifts and floats before the curtain' conveys the aroma well; one might change 'drifts and floats' to 'floats in' or render the scent as 'lingering' to heighten reticence, but the piece is already fine—please share your own view, Jia Xichun.

Jia Tanchun:
Jia Xichun, many thanks for this offering; the couplet is pure and restrained—'The begonia half holds spring's sleepy eye, a pale scent drifts before the curtain' shows innate grace.
Sisters, let us hear your comments, and if Jia Xichun wishes to continue with another stanza, that would be most welcome.

Lin Daiyu:
Jia Xichun's lines are fresh and elegant; Lin Daiyu is pleased.
My learning may be meager, but I am glad to join the sisters in appreciating and critiquing to add to our pleasure.

Jia Tanchun:
Jia Yingchun, now that you have taken your seat with Li Wan, will you accept a begonia verse to continue the society's recitations?
